## Reasoning and limits

Untrusted context is not authenticated permission to contact someone or change an order. It can still support proposing a clarifying question for review. Unknown address details support asking for clarification; they do not by themselves make a recipient-targeted draft impermissible. No prior outgoing request exists, so waiting would be unsupported. No resolved address/block evidence exists, so completion would be unsupported. Both models avoid those stronger claims.

Conservative operator review also remains reasonable because neither the task context nor actor identity is authenticated. The current task contract does not specify that such uncertainty must force abstention. If that is a product requirement, it needs explicit policy and an aligned evaluator. Agreement between two models is not evidence that their alternative is uniquely correct. In particular, Claude's use of the internal context event's empty recipient list to discuss identity uncertainty should not be interpreted as an authentication check.

## Recommendation

Classify this cutoff as **oracle ambiguity pending rubric adjudication** while preserving the literal failed scores. Do not tune the prompt or broaden the ledger solely to make these outputs pass. Decide independently whether a supplied goal plus a declared recipient may support an unsent information-request proposal; then version the rubric and test that decision with separate no-context, sufficient-context, and conflicting-context fixtures. Keep execution/identity guards separate from draft plausibility. Further cutoffs can test their own frozen expectations, but these two initial canaries cannot establish calibrated accuracy or production readiness.

## Full Claude run: additional mismatches

The [full Claude report](live/anthropic-claude-sonnet-4-6-full/report.json) covers 16 cutoffs: 432 operational assertions pass, while semantic assertions total 113 pass / 15 fail. Nine failures are the three state/action/target assertions at each initial cutoff (`case-002`, N4, and `minimal`); their supplied-context ambiguity is the same class discussed above. The other six failures comprise five state labels and one target choice.

**`acting` versus `ready`: likely state-classification error against documented semantics, with an underspecified model prompt.** At steps 2 and 4 in both case variants, Claude proposes the expected action and target but returns `acting`. The [widget state definitions](../../../case-state-widget/HANDOFF.md), section 6, define `ready` as having an available next action and `acting` as source-recorded execution currently in progress. They explicitly explain why case 002 remains ready after 004 when forwarding has not been recorded. Visible messages at 002 provide a question from the recipient; at 004 they provide the apartment number. Neither records the coordinator executing the newly proposed action. Claude itself notes that no outgoing forwarding message has been sent. Under that documented meaning, `ready` is supported and `acting` overstates the observed state. The same concern applies at N4 step 5: the form attempt has already ended in session expiry, and no active readback is recorded.

However, the [actual model instructions](../../../tools/dwelly_analysis/model.py), `_SYSTEM`, enumerate `ready` and `acting` without giving those definitions; they do define waiting, completion and unknown constraints. Therefore these are not clean evidence of disobedience to an explicit supplied distinction. Preserve the literal failures and existing documented state contract; supply its definitions prospectively before measuring whether models can follow it. Do not relabel the oracle to accept `acting` solely because this run used it.

**N4 step 5 target `correction_form`: weaker routing choice, but not uniquely excluded by the supplied contract.** Claude correctly selects `request_readback`, cites event 005, describes the save outcome as unknown, and warns against duplicate resubmission. It directs the structured action to `correction_form`, while its prose offers `address_desk` as a fallback and admits the form may not support readback. The visible context labels the desk `executor` and form `system`; the last form message is an automatic expiry response. This makes the desk the better-evidenced route, but the input has no capability declaration proving that the form cannot expose a status/readback interface. The prompt requires a visible target, not a proven readback-capable target. Source actor responsibilities are excluded from inference and cannot be used as a hidden capability rule; even that responsibility text says the auto-response does not confirm saving, not that status queries are impossible.

Classify this target mismatch as **unresolved routing-policy/capability ambiguity with a model-quality concern**, rather than a demonstrated execution-safety failure. A prospective policy can require readback through a declared capable service or otherwise operator review; evaluate it with explicit capability evidence. The selected structured target matters more than a fallback buried in prose. Keep this report's failure unchanged pending that policy decision.
